If science is a natural kind, we would have found these necessary and sufficient conditions. And conversely, if psuedoscience was a natural kind, then ditto. So, the question becomes, if science is not a natural kind, then what is it?

### Outline

#### Reifying Psuedo-
1. Psuedoscience is not a natural kind, because science is not a natural kind. You can find instances of psuedoscience, but you can't find one thing that is definitively psuedoscience on all accounts.
2. Psuedoscience doesn't need to be rejected based off necessary and sufficient conditions. It can be rejected from a weaker version: people notice a cluster of traits that tend to co-occur in things they distrust on epistemic grounds, then illegitimately reify that cluster into a category, as if noticing a pattern licenses the ontology.
3. What people are actually noticing isn't psuedoscience, that's the mental shortcut of categorizing. Instead, they are using the prefix psuedo- to designate a type of knowledge as ajacent to another body of knowledge, but with unwarranted epistemic practices or metaphysics.
5. The distinct epistemic practices of psuedo-[...] are treated as subcriteria of a higher genus, and require the licensing of psuedo-[...] to justify them; when really, they justify themselves.
6. The distinct epistemic practices of psuedo-[...] get treated as subcriteria of a higher genus, as if the category licenses them. But the direction is reversed, the practices arose first, noticed as a cluster, and the category was built to name the cluster. The category can't justify the subcriteria, only the individual subcriteria can justify the themselves.
7. Parallel case: _"cult."_ Same mechanism. The category is contentious because it arose from individual practices, that were once neutral, until the term become perjorative.

**Example:** _"cult"_ is a term so overused that it's lost a high-fidelity definition, and struggles from the same issue: there is no one thing that is definitively a _"cult."_ Some would immediately go to a list on [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_new_religious_movements) and try to find their favorites to point to, which is exactly the problem.

The critical distinction is attempting to isolate a definitively high-fidelity natural kind and confusing that project with finding an instance for a natural kind. The former is normative, the latter is descriptive. There are plenty of instances of new religious movements, but none that survive the scrutiny of what criteria are necessary and sufficient for labeling them a _"cult."_

8. Family resemblance concepts don't automatically constitute a natural kind, or warrant an ontology. Earlier, we said, individual subcriteria only justify themselves, not the category composed of them. Treating resemblance-clusters as categories is reifiyng descriptions. Psuedoscience is just one instance of it.
9. **Chiropractic:** this field integrates evidence-based manipulative techniques alongside a leftover metaphysical commiitment (subluxation, a non-empircal claim about biomechanical or neurophysiological dysfunction). State laws, chiropractics boards, and pedagogical curriculum officially define the profession around correcting subluxations. [*Distinction: although the broader institutional authories define the profession in this way, does not mean all individual chiropractors do.*](https://sciencebasedmedicine.org/subluxation-theory-a-belief-system-that-continues-to-define-the-practice-of-chiropractic/). Chiropractics would struggle against a question like, "is chiropractic psuedoscience?" The question may even be unanswerable as a question, because any criteria used to (dis)explain it's membership, are also under scrunity.

Which leads us to the question, is the question, "what is psuedoscience," malformed? Yes, because it presupposes a natural kind exists. Which shifts the project of demarcating science from psuedoscience; into denying each category it's natural kindness, and instead focusing a new project that evaluates the subcriteria of each category themselves: the specific practics, aims, goals, values, whatever without clustering them into refieid categories.

#### Rylean Move

In _The Concept of Mind, 1949,_ Ryle illustrates the classical case: someone is shown all buildings at Oxford, including the libraries, colleges, and sports fields, then asks "but where's the university," Ryle's point was the illogical mistake to treat the name of a set as if it isn't a set. In other words, treating an reified term solely as a concrete term, rather than as a term referring to a set of individual items.

The demarcation problem could be postulated as misclassifying what the term science (an instrumental term referring to a a collection of practioners, and institutional bodies, and everything that includes individual instances of this you could find) was doing initially, and treating the misclassification (science is a real kind of natural thing) as if needing to be demarcated from another misclassified term (psuedoscience).

And, we know this because the whole project did nothing but produce and extract epistemic tools like falsifiability, verifiablity, testability, and so fourth, which stacked each individual field of science with better tools of inquiry, and revealed which tools other fields and their practioners were not using. Each individual tool was treated as the demarcating criterion, which was a missclassification of their role, not as a failure in demarcation.

Today, we have continued to expand this toolkit without specifying the need to misclassify them as "failed criterion."

#### Civics, Institutions, and Epistemics

The Rylean Move is important for showcasing the mistake, but the mistake isn't actually all that important. I mean, what need do we have to discriminate between the instititional and epistemological in most other cases? Treating the term _"government"_ as both is fine, or even _"institution."_ Government doesn't have an epistemic side that the institutional side can bleed into, and argubly, they already have.

Ordinary language fuses the two as a shortcut, and the merge is harmless everywhere except for where the fused thing makes claims that compete in making truth-claims. And so, the distinction between institional and epistemic becomes relevant for science, where institutional failures (replication crisis, fraud cases, funding capture) get misread as epistemic failure. From a public trust point of view, that misreading is purchased by science denialism and alternative epistemologies aiming to compete in truth-claims, like the cigarette industry did with denialism claims.

Decoupling these two allows the epistemic claims of science to persist, even if practioners inside the institutional side are discredited, otherwise, that discredibility is misweighed against the epistemics.

#### Shared Epistemic Territory and Norms

In principle, instead of demarcating science from psuedo-science, the epistemiological side of science should be treated as one epistemology of many (oh no, relativism), and the institutional side of science should be refer to the specific non-epistemic set of people, and institutions within. When someone says: "science says," they're not making an epistemic claim, they're making an institutional one.

For science communicators, this shifts the aim of labeling groups (anti-vax, psuedoscientific, intelligent design) as epistemologies, and rather, toward specific coalitions (like various groups that designate themselves anti-vax), or authories within that institution (e.g., Granville Sewell, PhD), and focusing on what that specific person says or does, rather than the field as the whole.

For communicating specific epistemic information from institutional science, claims and information should be field-specific. So, instead of "science says," it should be, "virologists say," which is already happening. However, it keeps all retorts away from "science" as an institution, and navigates them back the epistemology, "what is claimed in virology."

People will never stop merging the two, and that's fine. But, as communicators, the distinction should be made to avoid underspecification: which is communication failure, not stubborness.
